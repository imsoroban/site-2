Why Linux Is Winning the OS War

October 30, 2026

Windows 10. God, how amazing Windows 10 was. Possibly one of the greatest operating systems out there, beating macOS and nearly every Linux distribution at the time. It was, in my opinion, a good place for Microsoft to be in the software world, a huge userbase, great performance, and a genuinely good experience. Microsoft had it all in the operating system world, what else could they want? Windows 10 was the undisputed king at the time. But as we learn from our life experiences, nothing lasts forever, and this was especially true for Microsoft and the release of Windows 11. Windows 11 was essentially Windows 10, but on steroids, and a lot more bloated. Windows 11 used a significantly higher amount of resources on release, almost a gigabyte of ram on average, and as time went on, this only worsened. More recently, with the release of LLMs (Large Language Models), such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok, Microsoft thought it would be a good idea to capitalise on the new found technology, launching Copilot. When you use Windows 11, Copilot is everywhere, from the start-up menu, their built in web browser, and there's even a button for it on their newer Surface laptops.

To me, Windows 11's release was the true downfall of Microsoft's share of the operating system market, and with such a horrible operating system, the company gained the nickname "Microslop". Microsoft has lost a huge portion of their developer community to a more advanced operating system, known as Linux. Linux banked on one factor to beat out Microsoft, and that was freedom. In Windows, you couldn't develop your own system based on it, it was locked down completely, using proprietary code and a copyrighted system. Meanwhile, the entire purpose of Linux is that you are completely free on your own system, leading developers to create their own Linux-based operating systems, such as Ubuntu, Mint, Arch, and Fedora, just to name a few.

Windows locks you down, with safety features in place to make sure that you can't break your system, or fully control it. On Linux, if you want to delete your entire file system, it's gone. Microsoft collects telemetry, and a lot of it too, even going so far as to be sued multiple times over privacy issues, including lawsuits over child privacy. On the other hand, Linux as a kernel does not collect any telemetry, it can only collect your data by the apps you install, such as your browser.

If you were to step into any tech/developer communities and talk about Linux, one of the main points people will mention is the support of games on Linux. For most people, this is probably the biggest downside, as you cannot play games such as Valorant, Overwatch, League of Legends or even Call of Duty. Although this is an issue, tools such as Proton and Wine have revolutionised the compatibility of many games, and with the popularity of the Steam Deck and Steam Machine, running on SteamOS, around 90% of Steam games are completely playable.

It really begs the question of what the point of an operating system is, should it be a lightweight tool that respects the privacy of the user, or a piece of software fighting for your data and the ability to monetise you. While Linux isn't perfect, in my opinion, I believe it is superior to Windows, and right now is the easiest time to jump ship. If you're interested but don't want to commit right now, Linux will only keep improving and make it easier to switch.
